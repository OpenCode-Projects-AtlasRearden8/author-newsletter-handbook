# Signup Form Copy

> Copy-paste text for the signup form and the page the reader sees
> around it. Chapter 2's job is the provider; chapter 3's checklist
> switches the settings; this file writes the words. Replace every
> [bracket].

## The three sentences every form needs

1. **What arrives:** what the reader actually gets (not "my updates" --
   the thing itself, in plain nouns).
2. **How often:** the real cadence from chapter 7, promised in human
   terms.
3. **The exit:** unsubscribing is one click and costs nothing -- stated
   in the welcome email, implied by the footer, never hidden.

If your form copy says all three honestly, consent is real and GDPR
checkboxes feel natural rather than legal armor.

## Form A: the friendly default (fits most providers' signup widget)

```text
Headline: [The promise in its plainest words. e.g., "AI explained like a friend would"]
Body: One email a month with [the concrete goods: e.g., "free tools,
      plain-English explainers, and notes from writing my books"].
      No spam, no selling your address, unsubscribe anytime.
Button label: [something honest and human: "Send me the next one" -->
      or your provider's default "Subscribe" if you like blunt]
```

> Prefer the specific button variant when your provider allows custom
> labels -- "Send me the next one" outperforms "Submit" because it
> names the actual transaction. (Shared as a design habit, not a
> guaranteed conversion claim.)

## Form B: the story-rich version (for a book profile or about page)

```text
Headline: Twice the [reader-relevant noun], zero the [hated thing].
          e.g., "New pages, no algorithm."

Body: Hi, I'm [your name]. I'm a [nurse turned AI author / your honest
      line]. Once a month I write [what the issue contains], including
      [one signature segment]. Join [reader count OR omit the number]
      other readers and reply anytime -- I read everything.

Button label: [Same honest variant as Form A]
```

> Note the bracketed "[reader count OR omit the number]": never be
> tempted to exaggerate the number hiding there. An honest "other readers"
> phrasing beats manufactured hype, and chapter 8's whole method is
> built on deserving trust rather than borrowing it.

## Form C: the minimal one-liner (bios, back matter, profiles)

```text
For the free email [what it is] -- [link]. One [cadence] email, plain
English, no spam: [very short signup URL].
```

> Keep this under a single breath. It lives in tight spaces (nested
> bios, marketplace author pages), where the form copy would crowd out
> the thing the reader came there to do.

## Thank-you page (the step most authors skip)

After a reader confirms, their next page can be working for you:

```text
Headline: You're in.
Body: [What happens next: "Check your inbox for a confirmation email
      -- one click inside and you're fully aboard." Then the welcome
      email lands in a minute.]
Also here: [1-2 links: the best free resource, the book page]. And a
      reply door: "Reply to the welcome email and introduce yourself --
      I read every one."
```

## Adaptation notes

- **Localize the promise, not the structure:** the three sentences
  ("what arrives," "how often," "easy exit") survive every topic.
- Never promise a freebie you have not finished making; deliver it
  within the first welcome email or do not mention it (chapter 4).
- If your provider offers a GDPR consent checkbox: leave it on. Two
  seconds of friction for the reader, full consent-recording for you.
