# Issue Calendar

> A filled-in, 12-issue starter calendar pairing with
> [issue-calendar.csv](issue-calendar.csv); copy it, rename the titles
> to your own niche, and keep the rhythm. Chapter 7 explains the
> cadence philosophy behind it; chapter 5 pairs each send with a subject
> line from [the cheat sheet](subject-line-cheatsheet.md).

## How to use it

1. Open the CSV in any spreadsheet app. Columns: week, issue number,
   working title, one-line promise, subject draft, send day, CTA, and
   notes.
2. The example rows are themed for this handbook's project (plain-English
   AI for beginners); your own titles will differ -- the *structure* is
   what carries over: one promise per issue, one CTA per issue, notes
   that flow into the next issue.
3. Sends go out monthly (chapter 7's small promise). If you send
   weekly instead, the same twelve topics carry a twelve-week cycle:
   change nothing else.

## Reading the example plan

The example assumes a first year starting in a month you choose. Its
content arc, roughly: welcome and orientation (1-3), useful tools and
workflow beats (4-8), archives and community (9-10), and one honest
performance review (11-12) that hands the year to issue 13 without
ceremony.

Twelve issues is about a year at monthly cadence -- which means the
calendar's main honest message: a year is 12 good topics, not 12
perfect pressure-cooker topics. Two "process" issues (2 and 10) make
quiet months into content instead of guilt.

## When the year runs out

Start a new cut of the CSV (name it like the archive does: e.g.,
`issue-calendar-2027.csv`), reuse the pattern, and let the back catalog
keep doing what it does: old issues resurface as future topics, and
already-written material is already written (chapter 9's mirror files).
