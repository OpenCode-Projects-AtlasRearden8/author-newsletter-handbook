# Welcome Sequence Templates

> A ready-to-paste four-email welcome sequence. Copy each email, change
> every `[bracketed]` slot, delete anything you would never say across
> the street, and set the delays your provider asks for (chapter 4 has
> the how-to). The voice on purpose: like a person, not a brand mascot.

## How to use this file

1. Read [Chapter 4](../docs/04-welcome-sequence.md) once (10 minutes) --
   it explains why each email exists.
2. Copy the emails below into your provider's automation settings in
   order, delays: instant, 1 day, 3 days, 5-7 days.
3. Fill every bracket. Any slot you cannot fill is a slot the reader
   also cannot fill -- simplify until you can.
4. Subscribe with your own second address; read all four on a phone
   before calling it done.

---

## Email 1 -- instant: the delivery and the promise

```text
Subject: Here's what you asked for
Preheader: [The freebie/favorite link] -- plus what to expect from me.

Hi [first name if your provider collects it, else "there" is fine],

Thank you for signing up. Here's the thing I promised on the signup page:

[LINK OR ATTACHMENT DESCRIPTION]

You'll hear from me about [once a month / your cadence] with [the real
promise: e.g., "plain-English AI tools and notes from writing The
Massive AI Manual"]. No spam, no third parties, unsubscribe anytime and
I won't take it personally.

[Your name]
```

> Keep this one plain on purpose. It is opened to *use* something.

---

## Email 2 -- one day in: who you are

```text
Subject: [Your own name] is the one writing these
Preheader: Two minutes on who's behind this, and the best of what I've made.

Hi again,

In case the signup page felt formal, here's the short version: I'm
[your name], [one honest line about who you are and what you make -- e.g.,
"a nurse-turned-AI-writer, explaining technology like a friend would"].

If you want a starting point instead of waiting for the next issue,
here are the three things I'd point you to first:

1. [Link: your best free resource / checklist / chapter]
2. [Link: your book or main page]
3. [Link: your single favorite piece of writing so far]

Fair warning: I occasionally admit my mistakes here, because they're
usually the useful part.

[Your name]
```

## Email 3 -- three days in: the question

```text
Subject: What are you trying to figure out?
Preheader: One question, and I read every answer.

Hi,

Quick honest question, since this goes to a real inbox I actually
check: what are you trying to make sense of right now?

[A specific, small choice, tailored to your topic -- e.g., "AI tools,
writing, or just keeping the humans fed this week?" / "The free tools,
the setup steps, or the part where you decide if any of it is worth
your time?"]

Hit reply and tell me in a sentence. I read every reply, and the
topics get picked by what people say.

[Your name]
```

> If a reply arrives, answer it with one genuine sentence within a day.
> Chapter 4's whole chapter exists to cause this email.

---

## Email 4 -- five to seven days in: expectations and the archive door

```text
Subject: What you can count on from me
Preheader: The schedule, the archive, and a small save-this request.

Hi,

Last welcome note, then we settle into [your cadence]. Three things
worth knowing:

1. The schedule: [once a month, first Tuesday-ish] -- you can count on
   this or count on a short explanation of why a month went dark.
2. The archive: past issues live at [archive link / your site URL], so
   nothing you missed is truly gone.
3. A small practical tip if you're the list-ish type: add this address
   to your contacts (or star the sender) and you'll never miss an
   issue to a spam folder again.

Glad you're here.

[Your name]
```

## Adaptation notes

- **Beehiiv's free tier and other one-automation limits:** run only
  Email 1 and expand when the list justifies paid.
- **If you have no freebie:** Email 1 becomes the archive door itself:
  "Here are three favorite issues to start with." Do not invent a
  stalling freebie to fill a slot.
- **Voice check before scheduling:** read Email 2 out loud once. If
  anything sounds like a stranger wrote it, rewrite that sentence
  yourself -- chapter 6's loop will help on the next content round,
  but the welcome sequence deserves your own fingers the first time.
