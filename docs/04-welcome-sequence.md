# The Welcome Sequence

The welcome sequence is the set of automatic emails that greet every new
subscriber the moment they sign up. It is the highest-leverage writing a
newsletter exists, because it is sent to someone at the exact moment their
interest explains itself: they *just asked for you*.

One plain fact to start: your welcome email will typically be the most-
opened email you ever send -- new subscribers routinely open it at rates
two to three times higher than your regular issues. Nothing else you build
gets this kind of attention. Build it once (today, from the template) and
it greets people forever.

## What the sequence is for

Every automatic email does two jobs: keep the promise the signup form made,
and set expectations for what arriving regularly will feel like. Done
badly, a welcome sequence reads as "and now, funnel." Done well, it reads
as a friendly neighbor who also happens to have a book.

The arc that works for authors -- and that the
[copy-paste template](../templates/welcome-sequence-template.md) follows:

| #  | When      | Job of the email                                                  |
| -- | --------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1  | Instant   | Deliver the promise (the link, the freebie, the archive) and say what to expect |
| 2  | 1 day in  | Who you are, what you make, the 2-3 things worth reading first     |
| 3  | 3 days in | First real engagement: one genuine question, inviting a reply       |
| 4  | 5-7 days  | Expectation-setting and the gentle cadence note; the archive door   |

Four emails is the whole recommendation. Two is fine. Zero is not --
that is not a welcome, just silence with a subscription attached.

## Email by email, what to write

**Email 1: the delivery + promise.** If your signup form promised
anything, it arrives here: a link to your free chapter, checklist, or
simply your site. Say thank you once, tell them exactly what is enclosed,
then set expectations in one line: "You'll hear from me about once a
month with plain-English AI tools and book news." Skip cleverness. This
email is opened to *use* something, not be impressed.

**Email 2: who you are, briefly and honestly.** Two short paragraphs:
your story in your own words (the "nurse turned AI author" shape is enough
-- resist a resume), and 2-3 links worth a reader's time: your best free
resource, your book page, your favorite issue. The job is "here is what
reading me gets you."

**Email 3: the question.** One specific, easy question tied to why they
subscribed: "What are you trying to make sense of right now -- AI tools,
writing, or just keeping the humans fed?" Receiving replies does more for
your long-term writing (and chapter 7's morale) than any growth tactic.
Reply to every reply with a sentence. Real human replies also quietly
teach the inbox filters that you are a welcome guest, not a robot.

**Email 4: expectations, kindly.** Restate the cadence, show where the
archive lives, invite them to set a filter or star the sender if they like
keeping things. This email quietly lowers unsubscribes for the next
year, because most unsubscribes are really "this person post too much" or
"forgot who this was." You just fixed both ambiguity losses.

## Rules that keep it human

- **Write the From name and header as a person.** "Dylan" beats "The AI
  Gazette" in every welcome metric.
- **One idea per email.** A welcome sequence is a conversation laid out
  in time, not four blog posts wearing email skin.
- **Every email ends with one next step** (read this, reply with this,
  save this). Two next steps make a to-do list; to-do lists get closed
  unread.
- **Plain text beats design here.** Welcome emails win when they read
  like a person. Logo-topped gradients read like a machine that has
  already sold you something.
- **Never fake scarcity or use countdown timers in a welcome sequence.**
  Chapter 8's reader is meeting you for years; the first-week tone is a
  loan at interest.
- **Do not paste private reader details into any AI tool.** Chapter 6 has
  the full policy; the one-line rule: AI drafts you, not names.

## Using AI to draft it (preview)

The full loop lives in chapter 6, and the welcome sequence is its best
demonstration case: welcome sequences are the easiest content in a
newsletter to sound canned ("Welcome aboard! We're thrilled to have
you..."), and AI will happily produce 300 words of that per email. The
template in [the templates folder](../templates/welcome-sequence-template.md)
starts from real, somewhat tired-sounding human shapes on purpose; you
edit by deleting anything you would never say across the street. The
one-line test: if a line could appear in any email from anyone, delete it.

## Setting it up, provider-agnostically

Almost every provider from chapter 2 has three names for this: `welcome
email`, `automated email`, or `form response email`. The steps:

1. Open the providers' **Forms** or **Automation** section.
2. Create a sequence (MailerLite and Kit both call this a workflow or
   sequence; Beehiiv's free tier limits you to one automated email -- run
   just email #1 there and expand when it earns it).
3. Paste the four emails from
   [the template](../templates/welcome-sequence-template.md), setting the
   delays: instant, 1 day, 3 days, 5-7 days.
4. Subscribe yourself with the test persona from chapter 3 and read all
   four emails on your phone, walking around the block. If a sentence
   reads badly out of a pocket, it reads badly everywhere.

## The one metric for the whole sequence

Not opens. Not clicks. **Replies to email #3.** Replies start
relationships; everything else in this chapter was machinery to make room
for them. Aim for any replies at all in the first month; if it is zero,
rewrite email 3's question to be a smaller, more specific ask, and try
again on the next batch of subscribers.
